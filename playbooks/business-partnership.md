# Partnership, B2B, and integration inquiries

## When this applies

Someone is writing as a company, brand, agency, or independent creator — not an
end user with a product question — proposing some kind of business relationship:
bulk/studio licenses, white-labeling, SDK/API access, integrating Filteronme into
their own product, sponsorship/brand collab, acquisition of Filteronme or of their
own project, press/review requests, or "let's promote each other."

**Tell a real inquiry from partnership-flavored spam:**
- Real: names a specific, checkable product or company; describes a concrete use
  case (a streaming platform, a stadium event, a Webex integration, a specific
  headcount of licenses); comes from a role-relevant address (company domain,
  named title); asks a real business question (pricing, licensing mechanics,
  technical requirements).
- Spam-flavored (route to the spam-outreach playbook instead, don't draft a
  qualifying reply): generic "we'd love to explore synergies" language with no
  concrete product; guest-post / "external content partnership" / SEO-review-site
  pitches (conv 3376435745, conv 3375834087); vague "let me share an overview of
  our dev services" cold outreach with no specifics (conv 3263462971); unsolicited
  "acquisition opportunity" pitches for a stranger's side project (conv 3339481160).
  These often share tells with cold sales outreach: mass-mailer tone, unsubscribe
  footers, no reference to Filteronme specifically beyond the product name.

When genuinely unsure which bucket it's in, it's cheap to ask one qualifying
question rather than guess — see below.

## What to check first

- Do they name a **real product or company**? Is there a **link** (site, app,
  YouTube demo, LinkedIn)? A press contact with a named outlet (heise Mac & i,
  conv 3118431666) or a named Logitech employee (conv 2730380389) is real even
  with a short message — don't require length, require specificity.
- Is there an actual **use case** described (streaming platform needing OBS
  bypass, a stadium event needing custom filters, a Webex reseller needing API
  access) or just enthusiasm ("we're very interested in this product")?
- Scale signal: license count, seat count, student/user count, or budget
  mentioned unprompted is a good sign this is a real opportunity worth Eddy's
  time, not proof it should be accepted.

## Policy

These are Eddy's calls, not the agent's. **The agent's only job is to draft the
qualifying-questions reply and tag `needs-human` — every single time, no
exceptions, even for Logitech.** The agent never commits to pricing, discounts,
API access, custom filter builds, affiliate terms, or white-labeling — Eddy
decides deal-by-deal whether to engage further, and history shows he often says
no even to large real companies (Cisco reseller work-mation, conv 2472863169;
custom-filter request from Great Big Events' colleague, conv 2336544976).

If it's clearly spam-flavored per the check above, don't draft a qualifying
reply — flag per the spam-outreach playbook instead (typically no reply, or a
one-line decline) and still tag `needs-human` only if there's real ambiguity.

## How to respond

Ask exactly one or two pointed questions, matched to what's missing. Examples
from history, anonymized:

> Hi, could you please provide more information about your product? Can you
> share a link?
>
> Do you have an approximate monthly budget?
>
> Best

(For an OBS-based streaming product wanting to bundle Filteronme in — conv
2551235298 pattern.)

> Hi [Name],
>
> Happy to discuss more. Could you describe your use case a bit more and how
> many licenses you would need?
>
> Please let me know if you have any custom requirements.
>
> Best,

(For a company wanting several licenses for a live-event/business use case —
conv 2274284646 pattern.)

For affiliate-style pitches, the qualifying ask is different — social proof,
not budget:

> Hi! Yes we do — if you're interested please share your social media links or
> plans on how you're going to promote Filteronme.
>
> Best

(conv 2615047358 pattern — Eddy later declined once he saw the traffic was
small: "I don't think it's a fit at the moment but will let you know if we
plan to expand our affiliate program.")

For press/review-code requests (conv 3118431666), the qualifying question is
usually unnecessary — flag for Eddy with a note like "press asking for a
review code, want to send one or skip?" rather than drafting outreach language
yourself.

## Escalate instead (tag needs-human) when

Always. This entire topic is escalate-with-a-draft, not escalate-instead-of.
Post the qualifying-question draft (or, for spam-flavored/press cases, a short
note summarizing the ask) as a private note and tag `needs-human` so Eddy sees
it and decides whether it's worth his time — including for recognizable big
names (Logitech, conv 2730380389), which he may simply choose not to answer.

## Notes from history

- Genuine bulk/studio license requests exist (cam studio wanting 17 seats,
  conv 2964059041; events company wanting 4–5, conv 2274284646) — Eddy's
  answer on licensing mechanics was simple: it's per-user-email, not a
  device lock, so N licenses just means N logins.
- Custom filter work has a real (if informal) pricing model when Eddy engages:
  "additional monthly fee, depends on complexity, send a screenshot/video for
  a quote" — but he's also declined similar requests outright when he judged
  bandwidth too limited (conv 2336544976, same client family as 2274284646).
  Don't treat "we did it once" as a standing offer.
- API/white-label/SDK requests (Cisco Webex reseller, conv 2472863169) have
  historically been declined for lack of engineering bandwidth, not price —
  the agent should not imply API access is coming.
- Big-name inbound (Logitech) and dubious "acquisition opportunity" pitches for
  a stranger's own project (Camerascii) both sat without a visible reply in the
  export — silence/close appears to be an acceptable outcome here; don't treat
  "no reply drafted" as an error condition for this topic the way it would be
  for a support question.
- A one-off "beer brand wants white-label" inbound (conv 3330944318) is the
  same shape as Logitech: flag it, let Eddy decide, don't chase a marketing
  contact that doesn't exist.
